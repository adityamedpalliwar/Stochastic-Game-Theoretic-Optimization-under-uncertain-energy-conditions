💡 The Core Motivation: Why do we need this game?

Traditional electric utilities create a distorted 22¢/kWh tariff spread: they charge households $0.30/kWh to buy electricity from the grid, but only credit $0.08/kWh when rooftop solar is exported. When a sunny household exports surplus energy, their next-door neighbor buys electricity from the grid at nearly 4× the price! Furthermore, midday solar flooding causes local transformer over-voltage (the "duck curve").

Our Solution: By pairing each household with a 5.0 kWh battery and coordinating trades through a multi-agent dynamic pricing game, neighbors trade surplus energy directly at an incentive-compatible price between $0.08 and $0.30/kWh. The 22¢ spread stays inside the community, yielding $10,559.67 AUD in direct savings.

The 4-Step Computational Pipeline

STEP 1
Monte Carlo Uncertainty
Takes 365 days of real Ausgrid solar and load panel data. Uses smoothed block bootstrapping with Gaussian noise to generate 1,000 synthetic days (24,000 rounds) preserving microgrid weather correlation.
STEP 2
Battery BESS Dispatch
Each household's 5.0 kWh battery executes greedy self-consumption: surplus solar charges the battery first; deficits draw from the battery first. Only unabsorbed energy enters the P2P market.
STEP 3
Dynamic Price Discovery
Aggregate community demand pressure λ = Dpool / Spool dynamically sets clearing price PP2P ∈ [$0.08, $0.30] AUD/kWh, guaranteeing Pareto-dominant coordination.
STEP 4
Counterfactual Benchmarking
Rigorously compares 4 operational modes: Pure Grid vs. Solar-Only vs. Battery-Only vs. Proposed P2P Game, isolating the exact dividends of solar, storage, and software coordination.

How Scenarios & Trajectories are Generated

Understanding the mathematical generation of stochastic renewable trajectories: smoothed block bootstrap, diurnal variance, and physical non-negativity laws.

📐 The Scenario Generation Pipeline Explained (Smoothed Block Bootstrap)
  • 1. Whole-Day Vector Sampling: Rather than sampling individual hours independently, we sample entire 24-hour day vectors across all 5 households simultaneously. This preserves the cross-household weather correlation (if Sydney has cloud cover at 1:00 PM, all 5 homes experience reduced solar).
  • 2. Hour-of-Day Diurnal Variance: For each hour $h \in [0, 23]$, we calculate historical standard deviations $\sigma_D(h)$ and $\sigma_S(h)$.
  • 3. Gaussian Noise Injection: Synthetic weather variance is introduced via $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, (0.15 \cdot \sigma)^2)$.
  • 4. Physical Constraints: We enforce non-negativity ($D \ge 0, S \ge 0$) and strictly clamp solar generation to 0 at night whenever historical base solar is zero.

❓ Core Clarification: How Trajectories are Averaged to obtain the Final Output

Is the output for a day obtained by taking the average of 1,000 trajectories?
YES, EXACTLY. Each of the 1,000 Monte Carlo scenarios represents one full 24-hour day across the 5 households. For each day $s \in [1, 1000]$, the system simulates all 24 sequential hourly game rounds, accumulating the daily cost: $$\text{Daily Cost}_s = \sum_{h=0}^{23} \text{Hourly Cost}_{s,h}$$ The headline daily numbers reported in our thesis and dashboard (e.g. $26.36/day Pure Grid vs. $15.80/day Proposed Game) are the exact arithmetic mean of those 1,000 daily totals: $$\text{Average Daily Cost} = \frac{1}{1000} \sum_{s=1}^{1000} \text{Daily Cost}_s$$ Similarly, the 24-hour diurnal curves plotted in the chart above represent the hour-by-hour average across all 1,000 trajectories, where the shaded envelope reflects the 10th and 90th percentile bounds across the 1,000 simulated days.

Physical Battery Dispatch & Dynamic P2P Pricing

How local battery energy storage systems (BESS) charge during the midday solar surge, discharge during evening load peaks, and how dynamic market pricing is discovered.

🔋 Physical Battery Specifications & Greedy Pre-Game Dispatch
• Capacity: 5.0 kWh per home
• Max Power: 2.5 kW charge/discharge
• Round-trip Efficiency: 95.0% (η ≈ 97.5%)
• Safe Operating SOC: 10% to 90% (0.5 to 4.5 kWh)

Pre-Game Greedy Dispatch Rule: Solar surplus charges the battery first up to 90% SOC. Household deficits draw from the battery first down to 10% SOC. Only the unabsorbed net surplus (Snet) or unmet net deficit (Dnet) enters the peer-to-peer trading pool.

24-Hour Battery Dispatch Dynamics (H001 Solar Powerhouse)
Initialized Half-Full (SOC = 50%) at 00:00
☀️ Midday Charging (09:00 – 12:00): As rooftop solar peaks, battery SOC climbs rapidly to 90% (maximum safe limit).
⚡ Trading Window (12:00 – 15:00): Once battery is full, excess solar spills into the P2P market as tradeable net surplus.
🌙 Evening Peak Shaving (17:00 – 22:00): The battery discharges stored solar energy to cover dinner demand, avoiding 30¢ grid imports!
Dynamic Price Discovery & Community Trading Pool
Uncapped Dynamic Pricing Model
Dynamic Price Formula:
$$\lambda = \frac{D_{\text{pool}}}{S_{\text{pool}} + \epsilon}, \qquad P_{\text{P2P}} = P_{\text{export}} + \left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\max}}\right) \cdot (P_{\text{import}} - P_{\text{export}})$$ When surplus solar floods the market (λ → 0), price drops to $0.08/kWh (cheap green energy for buyers). When demand dominates (λ ≥ 2.0), price rises toward $0.30/kWh (high earnings for sellers).
