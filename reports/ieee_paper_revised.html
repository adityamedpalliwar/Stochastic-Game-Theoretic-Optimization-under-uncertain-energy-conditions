Abstract—Rapid proliferation of residential rooftop photovoltaics and battery energy storage systems necessitates decentralized market mechanisms to overcome retail feed-in tariff distortions. This paper presents an integrated stochastic game-theoretic optimization platform for peer-to-peer (P2P) energy trading among heterogeneous prosumers coupled with independent 5.0 kWh physical battery energy storage systems. An uncapped dynamic demand-pressure pricing mechanism guarantees that the P2P clearing price strictly internalizes the retail tariff spread, providing Pareto-dominant economic incentives and establishing the existence of a unique pure-strategy Nash equilibrium. To quantify stochastic stability under compound meteorological and behavioral volatility, a smoothed block bootstrap Monte Carlo sampling framework is formulated, preserving empirical cross-household correlations across 1,000 independent 24-hour days (24,000 sequential hourly market rounds) drawn from real-world Ausgrid panel data. Four rigorous operational counterfactuals are evaluated: pure grid baseline, solar self-consumption, battery self-consumption without P2P, and the full proposed game-theoretic model. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework slashes total community electricity expenditures by 40.07% ($10,559.67 AUD saved over 1,000 days). Factor decomposition confirms that while solar (52.3%) and battery storage (42.6%) furnish substantial baseline arbitrage, P2P software coordination delivers an additional $540.34 AUD (5.1% of community savings, and up to 19.1% for high-demand consumers) with zero marginal hardware expenditure. Empirical profit distributions confirm strictly non-negative individual financial gains across all simulated scenarios, validating the platform robustness for next-generation transactive energy distribution networks.
Keywords—Peer-to-peer energy trading, game theory, battery energy storage systems, Nash equilibrium, dynamic pricing, Monte Carlo simulation, prosumer microgrid, uncertainty quantification.

I. Introduction

Distributed renewable energy resources, specifically residential rooftop photovoltaics (PV) and battery energy storage systems (BESS), are fundamentally restructuring modern low-voltage distribution grids [1]. Traditionally passive domestic electricity consumers are rapidly transforming into active prosumers capable of localized generation and load modulation [2], [3].

A primary barrier to efficient residential energy utilization is the substantial structural spread between retail import tariffs and feed-in export tariffs imposed by utility retailers [4]. In typical residential pricing schemes—such as those across the Australian National Electricity Market (NEM)—homeowners import electricity from the grid at approximately $0.30/kWh while receiving a feed-in credit of only $0.08/kWh for exported solar. This 22¢/kWh spread creates an economic distortion: prosumers with abundant midday solar generation receive negligible financial compensation, while adjacent non-solar neighbors are forced to import energy from centralized generation at full retail price.

Peer-to-peer (P2P) local energy trading has emerged as an attractive market framework to internalize this spread [5], [6]. By enabling direct, localized electricity transactions between prosumers and consumers within a distribution feeder, P2P markets allow prosumers to sell surplus energy at prices above the utility export credit (Pexport = $0.08/kWh) while offering consumers energy below the grid import rate (Pimport = $0.30/kWh). However, real-world residential microgrids operate under stochastic weather variations, unpredictable consumer occupancy patterns, and physical battery degradation constraints, making static or centralized optimization brittle [7], [8].

To address these challenges, this paper presents an integrated stochastic game-theoretic optimization platform for residential microgrids. The principal contributions of this work are as follows: (1) an N-player normal-form energy trading game coupling heterogeneous residential prosumers with independent 5.0 kWh physical battery energy storage systems; (2) an uncapped dynamic demand-pressure pricing mechanism guaranteeing Pareto-dominant coordination; (3) a smoothed block bootstrap Monte Carlo sampling framework evaluating 1,000 independent 24-hour days (24,000 sequential hourly market rounds) over real-world Ausgrid panel data; and (4) a rigorous four-mode counterfactual evaluation isolating the exact financial dividends of solar (52.3%), battery storage (42.6%), and P2P software coordination (5.1%).

II. System Architecture and Dataset

A. Microgrid Participant Topology

The modeled residential microgrid consists of N = 5 heterogeneous prosumer households (H001, H002, H003, H004, H005) connected to a common local distribution feeder and utility point of common coupling (PCC). Electricity transfers between neighbors are conducted over the local secondary network, while net aggregate deficits or surpluses are balanced with the primary utility grid.

B. Empirical Dataset Calibration

The load and generation profiles originate from the open-source Ausgrid Solar Home Electricity Dataset (Sydney, Australia) [12], which records General Consumption (GC), Controlled Load (CL), and Gross Generation (GG) for 300 homes at 30-minute intervals over a full year (2012–2013). Readings were aggregated into clean hourly intervals (Δt = 1.0 h).

III. Physical Battery Storage Formulation

A. Battery Energy Storage System Parameters

Each household possesses an independent physical BESS reflecting commercial lithium-ion systems: nominal energy capacity Ecap = 5.0 kWh, maximum charge/discharge power Pchmax = Pdismax = 2.5 kW, round-trip efficiency ηrt = 95.0% (ηch = ηdis = √0.95 ≈ 0.9747), safe operating state-of-charge bounds SOCmin = 10% (0.5 kWh) and SOCmax = 90% (4.5 kWh), initialized at SOC0 = 50% (2.5 kWh) at 00:00.

B. Pre-Game Greedy Dispatch Strategy

Battery dispatch is executed prior to market clearing via deterministic greedy self-consumption. Let Si,traw = max(0, Gi,t − Di,t) and Di,traw = max(0, Di,t − Gi,t).

1) Surplus Solar Charging:

Pch,i = min(Siraw, Pchmax, [SOCmax − SOCi]Ecap / [ηchΔt]) (1)
Sinet = max(0, Siraw − Pch,i) (2)

2) Deficit Load Discharging:

Pdis,i = min(Diraw, Pdismax, [SOCi − SOCmin]Ecapηdis / Δt) (3)
Dinet = max(0, Diraw − Pdis,i) (4)

3) Sequential SOC Update:

SOCi,t+1 = SOCi,t + [Pch,iηch − Pdis,i / ηdis] / Ecap (5)

IV. Dynamic Pricing Discovery and Game Formulation

A. Market Aggregate Pool Formulations

The community aggregate pool surplus and deficit are defined as:

Spool,t = ∑i=1N Si,tnet,   Dpool,t = ∑i=1N Di,tnet (6)

B. Dynamic Price Discovery Mechanism

The demand-to-supply pressure ratio is given by:

λt = Dpool,t / (Spool,t + ε) (7)

where ε = 10−6 kWh. The dynamic P2P clearing price is:

PP2P,t = Pexport + (λt / λmax)(Pimport − Pexport) (8)

bounded within [Pexport, Pimport] = [$0.08, $0.30] AUD/kWh with λmax = 2.0.

C. Market Clearing and Nash Equilibrium

Total physical P2P energy cleared is Qtrade,t = min(Spool,t, Dpool,t), transacted pro-rata among participants. Each prosumer minimizes net electricity cost:

Costi,t = mi,tPimport + qi,tbuyPP2P − qi,tsellPP2P − ei,tPexport (9)

Because Pexport ≤ PP2P,t ≤ Pimport, Costi,tP2P ≤ Costi,tgrid holds strictly for all participants, proving that participation in the dynamic P2P trading market is a weakly dominant strategy and the all-trading profile forms a Pareto-dominant strict Nash equilibrium.

V. Monte Carlo Uncertainty Quantification

To evaluate stochastic stability, a smoothed block bootstrap Monte Carlo sampling framework is formulated. For each of 1,000 scenarios, an entire 24-hour day vector is sampled from the 365 historical days to preserve cross-household weather correlation. Gaussian perturbation scaled by diurnal standard deviation σ(h) is injected:

Di,h(s) = max(0, Di,h(ds) + εD),   εD ∼ 𝒩(0, [0.15σD]2) (10)
Gi,h(s) = max(0, Gi,h(ds) + εS),   εS ∼ 𝒩(0, [0.15σS]2) (11)

Nighttime solar is strictly clamped to zero if base generation is zero. Over 1,000 days (24,000 rounds), daily costs are summed over 24 hours, and final reported metrics represent arithmetic means across all 1,000 scenarios.

C. Diurnal Hourly Trading and Price Dynamics

Analysis of the 24-hour diurnal profile confirms that local trading concentrates between 11:00 and 15:00. Batteries reach their 90% SOC ceiling by 11:00, causing unabsorbed solar surplus to flood the pool. Market pressure λ drops below 0.5, clearing the P2P price at $0.11–$0.15/kWh. Between 17:00 and 22:00, stored battery reserves discharge to shave dinner demand peaks, avoiding premium $0.30/kWh grid imports.

D. Statistical Stability and Downside Risk

Evaluation of the empirical profit distributions across all 1,000 Monte Carlo scenarios reveals that daily P2P trading profit is strictly non-negative (minimum and 5th percentile Q05 = $0.0000/day). This guarantees individual rationality and zero downside risk under diverse meteorological conditions.

VII. Conclusion and Future Work

This paper presented an integrated stochastic game-theoretic optimization framework for peer-to-peer energy trading in residential microgrids with distributed battery storage. An uncapped dynamic price discovery mechanism internalizes the utility retail tariff spread while maintaining homeowner privacy. Full-horizon Monte Carlo simulation over 1,000 days (24,000 hourly rounds) demonstrated a 40.07% total electricity expenditure reduction, capturing $10,559.67 AUD in community savings. Factor decomposition proved that while solar (52.3%) and battery storage (42.6%) provide baseline savings, P2P trading delivers an extra $540.34 AUD (5.1%) pure software dividend with zero extra hardware cost, providing up to 19.1% of savings for heavy consumers.

Future work will extend the framework to incorporate distribution network voltage constraints, reactive power compensation, continuous double-sided auction bidding, and community scaling to 100+ prosumer nodes.
